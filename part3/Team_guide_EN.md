# Sense Reply case — Team guide to the final presentation

**Albert School BDD #3 · Case B1 · Version of 18 November 2026**

This guide explains every idea and every number in the final presentation (9 slides and 2 backup slides), so that each of us can answer any question from the jury. The last part explains the simulation tool in full detail.

**How to use it.** Read part 1 and learn the six numbers by heart. Read the slides you present in part 3. Skim the question bank in part 7 the evening before.

**Three kinds of numbers.** In this guide, *sourced* means it comes from a public source or a team document. *Our assumption* means we chose it ourselves. *Our proposal* means it is a price we recommend. Sense Reply gave us no price and no contract value.

## 1. The story in one minute

1. **The problem.** Reply invents the future, but others sell it. Today Reply runs a free pilot, signs a custom 3-year contract with no promised result, and earns from custom projects. When an innovation becomes standard, cheaper competitors sell it.
2. **Our answer.** Start in Italy, scale through partners, get paid every year.
3. **Who.** Multi-site, energy-intensive manufacturers in Northern Italy: about 860 named targets.
4. **How.** Two speeds on one platform. Reply keeps inventing with large groups (the frontier speed). What is proven becomes a managed service, sold plant by plant and through partners (the mature speed).
5. **The result.** For the same sales effort, revenue per consultant goes from €124k to €303k in year 3, and recurring revenue from 34% to 65%.

**The six numbers to know by heart**

| Number | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **€278 per MWh** | What Italian companies pay for electricity, 29% above the EU average of €216 | Sourced (team Part 1) |
| **About 860** | Italian manufacturers that fit our ideal customer; we aim to win about 25 in 3 years | Sourced list, filtered with our assumptions |
| **€52k a year** | What a typical plant gains with the platform, 3.8% of its electricity bill | Our model |
| **€18k a year** | What Reply earns per plant: €10k fixed plus 15% of the measured gain. The client keeps €35k | Our proposal |
| **€124k → €303k** | Revenue per consultant in year 3, today's model against ours | Our model |

## 2. The vocabulary in plain words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Edge gateway | A small industrial computer installed in the plant, connected to the machines, the solar panels and the meters |
| AI agent | A small program with one job (for example "use more of our own solar") that decides and acts on its own, inside limits set by people |
| Platform | The software that installs, updates and supervises the agents on all sites from one place |
| Digital twin | A live digital copy of each asset: what it should be doing against what it is doing. It lets us measure results before and after |
| Flexible loads | Machines whose consumption can be moved in time without harming production: cold rooms, ovens, compressors |
| Virtual battery | Using flexible loads like a battery. A cold room cooled a little more at noon needs less power in the evening |
| Managed service | Reply runs the plant's energy orchestration for a yearly fee. It is a service contract, not a software licence |
| Success bonus | A share of the savings that were actually measured. Upside only: if there are no savings, the bonus is zero |
| Baseline | The plant's behaviour recorded before the pilot. Savings are measured against it |
| Conditional pilot | A 3-month paid test with targets agreed in writing first. If the targets are met, the client signs |
| Fleet operator | A company that manages energy for many sites: ESCOs, aggregators, battery and solar integrators |
| ESCO | Energy service company. It carries out energy-saving projects for its clients |
| Aggregator | A company that groups the flexibility of many sites and sells it to the grid |
| White-label | A partner sells the service under its own brand. Reply stays invisible and receives a fee per plant |
| Lighthouse client | A large, well-known client used as a reference. Reply co-innovates with it |
| Recurring revenue | Revenue that comes back every year without a new sale |

## 3. Slide by slide

### Slide 1 — Title: "Reply invents the future. Here is how it sells it."

**Message.** One tension runs through the whole deck: Reply innovates, others earn.
**If asked "who are you?"** A team of Albert School students acting as business consultants for Sense Reply, the Reply group company for IoT in energy.

### Slide 2 — The problem: how Reply sells today

**Message.** Today's way of selling works, but it caps growth.

**The idea.** Three steps today:
1. A free pilot of about 3 months on one site.
2. A 3-year contract for the whole business, on a best-effort basis, with no promised result.
3. Revenue from custom projects and integration.

Three problems follow:
- **Value leaks to copycats.** When an innovation becomes standard, single-feature competitors sell it more cheaply.
- **Growth needs more consultants.** Every project is custom, so revenue grows only with headcount.
- **Free pilots create no commitment.** They tie up people with no guarantee that the client signs.

**Source.** Our meeting with Sense Reply on 13 November 2026.

**If asked "what do you keep?"** The pilot, integration, customisation for large clients and the innovation identity. We add to Reply's engine; we do not replace it.

### Slide 5 — Competitors: "Everybody sells a piece. Nobody sells the neutral brain"

**Message.** There is an empty seat in the market, and Reply can take it.

**The map.** Two questions place every competitor: is it open to any vendor's equipment (horizontal axis), and does it decide on site on its own (vertical axis)?

| Family | Examples | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Industrial incumbents | Siemens, Schneider Electric, ABB | Own the hardware, intelligent | Locked to their own equipment |
| Cloud plumbing | AWS, Azure | Open, cheap (about $0.16 per device per month) | No energy know-how |
| Utility-owned platforms | Next Kraftwerke (Shell), gridX (E.ON), Enel X | Access to energy markets | Owned by an energy seller |
| Battery makers' software | Bundled with the battery | Free, good for one battery | One asset, one brand |
| Status quo | Fixed rules, spreadsheets | Familiar, no perceived risk | Cannot react to prices or faults |
| Barbara (Spain) | — | The closest twin | Built for grid operators, not factories |

**Why neutrality is worth money.** Energy sellers bought the independent platforms: Centrica bought REstore (2017), Shell bought Next Kraftwerke (2021), E.ON took full control of gridX (2022). An ESCO will not run its clients on a rival utility's software. When Octopus spun out its software, Kraken, in 2025, it was valued at $8.65bn. Reply sells no electricity, so it can be the neutral platform: "Your clients, your data, your brand."

**Two more findings, not on the slide**
- **The real competitor is free.** Battery makers give basic software away. Most Italian sites have solar but no battery (only 91 MWh of commercial storage connected in Q1 2026). So we lead with flexible loads, the "virtual battery".
- **Everyone adds AI; few can prove what it did.** AI in critical infrastructure becomes "high-risk" under the EU AI Act from 2 December 2027. Reply logs every decision.

**Backup slide "Competitor matrix".** 11 players rated on 9 dimensions (our assessment from public information). Reply is the only one strong on edge intelligence, AI agents, multi-asset control, neutrality and fleet operations at once. Its two weak cells: no access to energy markets (solved with aggregator partners) and few industrial references (solved with lighthouse pilots).

### Slide 6 — Our model: "Two speeds, one platform. A typical plant gains about €52k a year"

**Message.** How the model works, and what it is worth for one plant.

**The two speeds**

| | Frontier (Reply as today) | Mature (new) |
|---|---|---|
| What | New capabilities | Proven agents, packaged |
| With whom | Large groups | Typical and mid-sized manufacturers |
| Who sells | Reply directly | Reply plant by plant, and partners |
| How Reply is paid | Project fee | Yearly fee plus a bonus on measured savings |

**The lifecycle.** Explore → Prove (frontier), then Package → Scale (mature). An idea is invented with a large client, proven in a pilot, packaged as a standard agent, then sold at scale. Reply finally earns when its ideas become standard.

**The plant.** 5 GWh a year, 1 MWp of solar on the roof, no battery. Its bill is 5,000 MWh × €278 = €1.39M.

| Case | Gain | Share of the bill | Paid to Reply | Kept by the client |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cautious | €24k | 1.7% | €14k | €10k |
| **Base** | **€52k** | **3.8%** | **€18k** | **€35k** |
| Optimistic | €94k | 6.8% | €24k | €70k |

The gain comes mostly from two things: using more of the plant's own solar (€20k) and moving consumption to cheaper hours (€23k). Part 4 gives the full calculation.

**Is it credible?** A study of 83 plants that set up an energy management system measured 3 to 4% a year (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory). Our base case, 3.8%, is in that range.

**The slider.** It changes the plant's size from 2 to 20 GWh. Part 8 explains it.

### Slide 7 — Price: "A fixed fee plus a bonus: Reply earns one third of the gain"

**Message.** A simple price list that keeps Reply's "no guarantee" position.

| Line | Price | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional pilot | €20k | At cost. Credited against set-up if the client signs |
| Set-up | €30k for the first plant, €12k for each further plant | Cheaper after the first because agents and connectors are reused |
| Managed service | €10k per plant per year | Reply runs the plant's energy orchestration |
| Success bonus | 15% of measured savings | Upside only, never a penalty |
| Through partners | €4k per plant per year, minimum 25 plants | White-label platform fee. The partner sells and installs |

**The rule behind it.** The client keeps about two thirds of the gain, Reply takes about one third. In the base case: €10k + 15% × €52k ≈ €18k for Reply, €35k for the client. The client gains about €3 for every €1 paid.

**The pilot.** Targets are agreed in writing first. If they are not met, the client has no obligation. No saving is promised in advance.

**"One partner is worth almost four direct accounts."** A partner growing from 25 to 50 to 75 plants pays about €200k a year on average. A typical direct account brings about €54k a year. 200 ÷ 54 ≈ 3.7, and Reply runs no pilot.

## 4. The numbers, step by step

### 4.1 What a plant gains (€52k)

The plant: 5 GWh a year, 1 MWp of solar that produces 1,100 MWh a year, of which 20% (220 MWh) goes to the grid today.

| Source of gain | Calculation, base case | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Using more of its own solar | Agents bring back half of the exported solar: 110 MWh. Each MWh used on site avoids buying at €278 instead of selling at €100: 110 × (278 − 100) | €19,580 |
| 2. Moving consumption to cheap hours | 10% of consumption can be moved: 500 MWh. Minus the 110 MWh already counted: 390 MWh × €60 price gap | €23,400 |
| 3. Lower peaks | Peak of 1,000 kW, reduced by 10%, at a network charge of €28.35 per kW per year | €2,835 |
| 4. Selling flexibility through an aggregator | 200 kW offered, at €47,000 per MW per year, 70% kept after the aggregator's cut | €6,580 |
| **Total** | | **€52,395** |

What changes between the three cases:

| Parameter | Cautious | Base | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of exported solar brought back on site | 30% | 50% | 70% |
| Share of consumption that can be moved | 5% | 10% | 15% |
| Price gap between expensive and cheap hours | €39 per MWh | €60 | €88 |
| Peak reduction | 5% | 10% | 15% |
| Flexible power offered, per GWh consumed | 20 kW | 40 kW | 60 kW |

## 5. Three rules of wording

1. **Never say "licence".** Say "managed service" and "white-label platform fee". Reply was clear that its contract is not a licence.
2. **Never promise savings.** Say "estimate", "measured savings", "upside only". Reply guarantees nothing, and neither do we.
3. **Never present Reply as a cheap software vendor.** Custom projects and co-innovation stay. The managed service comes on top.

## 7. Question bank

**About the problem and the answer**

1. *What does the platform actually do?* Small computers in the plant run AI agents that decide, in real time, when machines should run so that the plant uses its own solar, buys at cheaper hours and avoids peaks. A central platform installs and supervises the agents on all sites.
2. *Why is today's model a problem if it works?* It grows only by adding consultants, and the value of each innovation is captured by cheaper competitors once it becomes standard.
3. *How is your model different from today?* A paid conditional pilot, growth plant by plant, a yearly fee with a bonus, and partners. Reply scales without adding a consultant per client.
4. *Does packaging kill Reply's innovation identity?* No. The mature speed funds the frontier speed. Co-innovation with large groups stays.

**About clients**

5. *Why Italy first?* Highest prices, an emptier field, and Reply's home market. Germany is phase 2, timed to its 2029 grid-fee reform.
6. *Why manufacturers and not utilities?* Utilities score high but buy slowly, and many own competing platforms.
7. *Who signs at the client?* The CFO or the owner. The energy manager champions; the IT or security manager can block.
8. *Where does 860 come from?* From Italy's official list of energy-intensive companies, filtered by sector, size, region and profile.

**About competitors**

9. *Why would a client choose you over Siemens or Schneider?* "Keep your Siemens and Schneider equipment. We orchestrate everything around it." They are locked to their own hardware.
10. *Why not AWS or Azure?* "They give you the road. We give you the driver." They sell plumbing for cents, with no energy intelligence.
11. *What if a battery maker gives its software away?* We do not sell battery optimisation. We run the whole site, and most Italian sites have no battery.
12. *Why would a partner not build its own tool?* Neutrality, time to market, an audit trail ready for the AI Act, and no team to maintain.
13. *What stops competitors from copying the next innovation too?* They will. That is why Reply should earn on each innovation across its whole life, and sell a system that keeps gaining agents, not a single feature.

**About the price**

14. *Why pay a bonus if you guarantee nothing?* It is upside only. The client pays more only when savings are measured, against a baseline agreed in writing before the pilot.
15. *Is €10k per plant too expensive?* It is a service run by Reply's experts, not software. It is under 1% of the bill, and the client gains about €3 for every €1 paid.
16. *How do you measure the savings fairly?* The digital twin records the plant's behaviour before the pilot. That baseline is agreed in writing and reviewed once a year.
17. *Would Reply accept variable revenue?* The bonus is about 44% of the recurring revenue per plant in the base case. The fixed fee alone covers the service. This point should be confirmed with Sense Reply.

**About the numbers**

18. *Your numbers depend on your assumptions.* Yes, and they are all declared. In the worst case we tested, revenue per person is still about €230k against €124k today.
19. *Why does revenue per person matter?* A consulting firm sells its people's time. Earning more per person is how it grows without hiring at the same pace.
20. *Is €3M in year 3 not small?* It is the result of about 10 people on one segment in one country. The point is the revenue per person and the 65% that recurs, before Germany.

**About risk**

21. *Is it safe to let AI control energy assets?* Agents plan; fixed controls enforce safety limits; every action is logged. The AI never overrides the plant's safety rules.
22. *What about the EU AI Act?* From 2 December 2027, AI in critical infrastructure is treated as high-risk. Logging every decision is already built into the platform.
23. *What would make you stop?* Pilot conversion under 30% after 6 months, no partner signed, or a measured gain below 2% of the bill.
24. *Where is the Business Model Canvas?* In our written report (Part 3). In short: we keep large groups, custom projects and the consulting team; we add partners, packaged agents and three revenue lines.

## 8. The simulation tool, in full detail

There are two tools, built on the same calculations:

- **The web simulator** ("Plant Gain Simulator"), opened with the "Live demo" button: https://claude.ai/artifact/QL1t3dGBcL4TFUYGn9bzKT. It has three screens. A standalone copy also exists as a file (`plant-gain-simulator-standalone.html`).

### 8.1 Screen 1: "Plant simulator"

**Purpose.** Answer the client's question "what would my plant gain?" in front of them.

**Settings under "Your plant"**

| Setting | Default | Range of the slider | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|

Every value can also be typed in the box next to the slider.

**"How flexible is the plant?"** Three buttons: Cautious, Base, Optimistic. Each sets five hidden parameters at once (table in part 4.1).

**Settings under "The offer"**

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Managed service fee | €10,000 per plant per year | Fixed yearly fee |
| Bonus on measured savings | 15% | Reply's share of the measured gain |
| Set-up of the first plant ("More settings") | €30,000 | One-off |
| Set-up of each further plant | €12,000 | One-off |
| Pilot fee, credited if you sign | €20,000 | One-off |
| Price received for exported solar | €100 per MWh | What the grid pays for surplus solar |
| Solar output per MWp | 1,100 MWh a year | Yearly production of 1 MWp |

**Values fixed in the tool** (not adjustable on screen)

| Value | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Network charge on peak power | €28.35 per kW per year | e-distribuzione medium-voltage tariff, 2026 |
| Revenue for flexible power | €47,000 per MW per year | Italian capacity market, auction for 2027 |
| Share kept after the aggregator's cut | 70% | Our assumption |
| Peak power of the plant | 200 kW per GWh consumed | Our assumption (1,000 kW for 5 GWh) |

**The formulas**

1. Solar output = solar size × output per MWp
2. Solar exported = solar output × share sent to the grid
3. Solar brought back = solar exported × share brought back (30%, 50% or 70%)
4. **Gain 1, solar** = solar brought back × (electricity price − price received for exported solar)
5. Consumption that can be moved = electricity use × share that can be moved (5%, 10% or 15%)
6. **Gain 2, moving consumption** = (consumption that can be moved − solar brought back) × price gap (€39, €60 or €88). The subtraction avoids counting the same MWh twice.
7. **Gain 3, peaks** = electricity use × 200 kW × peak reduction × €28.35
8. **Gain 4, flexibility** = electricity use × flexible kW per GWh ÷ 1,000 × €47,000 × 70%
9. **Total gain** = gain 1 + gain 2 + gain 3 + gain 4
10. Electricity bill = electricity use × price. Share of the bill = total gain ÷ bill
11. **Paid to Reply** = managed service fee + bonus × total gain
12. **The plant keeps** = total gain − paid to Reply

| Solar used on site, target | 100% − share sent to the grid × (1 − share brought back) | 90% |

| Estimated gain over 3 months | Total gain ÷ 4 | €13,099 |

**The seven terms.** Scope (one plant, about 3 months) · baseline and targets agreed in writing before the start · price (the pilot fee, at cost) · if the targets are met, the client signs and the fee is credited against set-up · if not, no obligation, and Reply keeps the data with the client's consent · Reply promises best effort and a measured result, no guaranteed saving · after the pilot, the yearly fee plus the bonus, and one more plant each year.

The amounts in the text follow the settings of screen 1.

**How revenue is calculated, two-speed model**

- Pilot fees from clients that do not sign = pilots × (1 − conversion) × pilot fee
- Set-up = first plants × €30,000 + further plants × €12,000

### 8.6 Limits to state if asked

- The tool gives estimates from averages, not a forecast for a specific plant. The pilot measures the real figures.
- It assumes the plant has an hourly-priced electricity contract. Without it, gain 2 is smaller.
- It ignores batteries on purpose: most Italian sites have none, and battery software is given away.
- It does not count avoided breakdowns, which would add to the gain.
- Prices and conversion rates are ours. Reply's own figures can replace them in a few clicks.
