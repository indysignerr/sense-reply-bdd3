# Sense Reply case — 5-minute pitch

**Albert School BDD #3 · Case B1 · Rehearsal version, 19 November 2026**

About 700 words, 5 minutes at a calm pace. One block per slide, with its duration. Text in *[brackets]* is an action, not something to say.

---

### Slide 1 · Title · 15 seconds

Good morning. We are a team from Albert School, and for three weeks we worked as consultants for Sense Reply. Our whole work comes down to one sentence: Reply invents the future, but others sell it.

### Slide 2 · Problem · 40 seconds

Here is how Reply sells today. First, a free pilot of about three months. Then a three-year contract, on a best-effort basis, with no promised result. And revenue comes from custom projects.

It works. But it has three problems. When an innovation becomes standard, cheaper competitors sell it. Growth needs more consultants, because every project is custom. And a free pilot commits the client to nothing.

### Slide 3 · Answer · 30 seconds

Our answer: start in Italy, scale through partners, get paid every year.

Who? Multi-site manufacturers in Northern Italy. How? Two speeds on one platform: Reply keeps inventing with large groups, and turns what is proven into a managed service. And the engine is partners, who sell that service under their own brand.

### Slide 4 · Clients · 40 seconds

Why Italy? Italian companies pay 278 euros per megawatt-hour, 29 percent above the European average. So every megawatt-hour saved is worth more there.

We scored twelve segments. The best one is multi-site, energy-intensive manufacturers: two to ten plants, solar on the roof, and machines whose consumption can be moved in time. From Italy's official list, that gives about 860 named targets. Winning 25 in three years is realistic.

### Slide 5 · Competitors · 40 seconds

Who are the competitors? Everybody sells a piece. Siemens and Schneider are smart, but locked to their own hardware. Amazon and Microsoft are open, but bring no energy intelligence. And the independent platforms were bought by energy sellers: Shell, E.ON, Centrica.

That matters, because an energy service company will not run its clients on a rival's software. Reply sells no electricity. So it can take the neutral seat, and today nobody is sitting in it.

### Slide 6 · Our model · 60 seconds

So how does our model work? Two speeds. The frontier speed is Reply as it is today: new ideas, built with large groups, paid as projects. The mature speed is new: what is proven becomes a managed service, sold plant by plant.

What is it worth for a client? Take a typical plant: five gigawatt-hours a year, solar on the roof, no battery. Our agents run the machines when the sun shines and when electricity is cheap. In our base case, the plant gains about 52 thousand euros a year, close to 4 percent of its bill.

*[Click "Cautious"]* Even if we are too optimistic, the client still keeps about 10 thousand.

*[Move the slider to 10]* And a plant twice as big gains about 92 thousand.

### Slide 7 · Price · 30 seconds

How is Reply paid? A pilot at 20 thousand euros, with targets agreed in writing, credited if the client signs. Then 10 thousand per plant per year, plus 15 percent of the savings we measure. No savings, no bonus.

The client keeps two thirds of the gain; Reply takes one third. And partners pay 4 thousand per plant to sell it under their own brand.

### Slide 8 · Numbers · 35 seconds

What does it change for Reply? With the same number of pilots, revenue over three years goes from 3.3 to 5.2 million euros.

But the number that matters for a consulting firm is revenue per consultant. It goes from 124 to 303 thousand euros, because agents are reused and partners do the field work.

*[Click "Both"]* And even if only 30 percent of pilots convert and no partner signs, it is still 230 thousand.

### Slide 9 · Roadmap · 20 seconds

We do this in three phases over 36 months: prove, package, scale. And we stop and rethink if, after six months, fewer than 30 percent of pilots convert.

To close: Reply keeps inventing the future. With this model, it also sells it. Thank you.

## For the rehearsal

- **Time yourselves once, out loud.** Reading silently is always faster than speaking.
- **Decide who presents which slide** before you start, and say the hand-overs out loud.
- **Ask the reviewer three things at the end:** what he remembers in one sentence, which number made him doubt, and which slide he would remove.

## Two answers to have ready

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Where do your prices come from?" | They are our proposals. Sense Reply gave us no price. We built them from the value for the client: the client keeps two thirds of the gain. |
| "Your numbers depend on your assumptions." | Yes, and they are all declared. In the worst case we tested, 30 percent conversion and no partner, revenue per consultant is still 230 thousand euros against 124 today. |

## Words to avoid

Never say "licence" (say "managed service"). Never say "guaranteed" (say "measured savings"). Never present Reply as a cheap software vendor: custom projects stay.
