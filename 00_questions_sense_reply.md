# Smart questions — understand the case in depth & win the BDD

> Principle: a smart question **states our hypothesis and asks to be proven wrong**.
> "Who are your clients?" makes us look unprepared. "We think aggregators are a faster entry than DSOs because of the 18–36-month tender cycles. Where are we wrong?" makes us look like consultants, and the answer is far more useful.
>
> Each question shows **what it unlocks** (which deliverable or decision).

---

## Part 1 — To Sense Reply (the client)

### A. The real decision behind the case (ask first)

| # | Question | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | "Imagine our recommendation lands on your CEO's desk on 21 November. **What decision will they take with it in the next 3 months?** Budget, hiring, a first target client?" | Tells us what the deck must answer. Everything else is secondary |
| A2 | "**What would make you reject our recommendation**, even if the analysis were correct?" | Hidden constraints: Reply group politics, AWS partnership, existing clients |
| A3 | "Who inside Reply decides whether this becomes a **product with its own P&L** or stays a consulting accelerator? What do they need to see?" | The real audience behind the jury, and the core of the product vs service question |
| A4 | "Reply makes money by selling consultant days. **If the platform becomes self-serve, who loses revenue internally?** How is that resolved today?" | The cannibalisation conflict. Almost no team will raise it |

### B. Hypothesis tests on customers (Deliverable 1)

| # | Question | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | "Our hypothesis: **sell to fleet operators (aggregators, DSOs) before single sites**, because value grows with the number of gateways. Does your pipeline confirm or contradict that?" | Validates the core of our segmentation |
| B2 | "Walk us through **the last deal you won** and **the last one you lost**: who the client was, who signed, how long it took, why it ended that way." | Real ICP, buying centre, sales cycle, competitors, all in one question |
| B3 | "If you could send your sales team to **only one segment in 2027**, which one would you pick, and which one would you drop?" | Forces a trade-off. Their gut feeling vs our scoring |
| B4 | "Energy communities came up a lot at the kickoff. In Italy the average community is around 80 kW. **Who would actually pay you there:** the community, the utility, an ESCO, or the DSO?" | Tests our disagreement with the kickoff framing |
| B5 | "What is the **minimum number of gateways** below which a client is not profitable for you?" | Disqualification criterion for the ICP and an input for market sizing |
| B6 | "Which **trigger event** makes a client call you: a regulation deadline, a price spike, a grid-connection refusal, a cyber audit?" | Timing of the go-to-market; tells us which regulations really sell |

### C. Competition & differentiation (Deliverable 2)

| # | Question | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | "When a client says no, **what do they do instead?** Build in-house on AWS, keep their SCADA, buy from an OEM, or nothing?" | The real alternative is often "do nothing" or "build in-house", not a named competitor |
| C2 | "You said no equivalent platform exists. **Which product comes closest**, and what would it take for them to catch up: 6 months, 2 years?" | Durability of the advantage. Lets us challenge "unique" politely |
| C3 | "Your platform runs on AWS, and AWS sells IoT Greengrass. **Is AWS a channel, a partner or a future competitor?**" | Biggest strategic risk; also a possible channel (AWS Marketplace) |
| C4 | "What can you do **that a client cannot rebuild in 12 months** with AWS services and a good integrator?" | Forces them to name the real moat: CIM, security, agents, domain knowledge |

### D. Business model (Deliverable 3)

| # | Question | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | "On your last 3 projects, **what share of the effort was reusable platform and what share was custom integration?**" | The fact that decides "product or service". Without it, the BMC is opinion |
| D2 | "Our hypothesis is a **hybrid model**: a paid pilot/integration project first, then a recurring licence per gateway. What would break this model with your clients?" | Validates the revenue model |
| D3 | "Have you ever priced **on results** (share of savings or flexibility revenue)? What happened?" | Feasibility of outcome-based pricing |
| D4 | "For DSOs: does regulation make them prefer **capex (buy)** or **opex (subscribe)**? Does that change in Italy with the new regulatory framework?" | Pricing structure per segment |
| D5 | "What is a **realistic price range** for a comparable project: integration fee and yearly cost per site or gateway?" | Revenue scenarios and market size |

### E. Product reality (to avoid recommending something they can't deliver)

| # | Question | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | "What is **in production with paying clients today**, and what is still demo?" | Separates what can be sold now from roadmap |
| E2 | "Do agents **act** on assets or only **propose**? Under the AI Act, grid safety uses can be high-risk. How are you preparing?" | Regulatory risk slide; DSO go-to-market |
| E3 | "Why an **LLM** to choose between solar, battery and grid rather than classical optimisation? Do you have a measured gain?" | Credibility of the AI argument. Expect this question from a sharp jury |
| E4 | "How long does **onboarding a new site** take today, and what is the bottleneck?" | Scalability, cost structure in the BMC |

## Part 2 — To the school / jury (how to win)

| # | Question | Why |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | "Who sits on the **final jury**: Sense Reply people, professors, or both? What are their roles?" | Adapt the depth: business vs technical |
| J2 | "What is the **grading grid**? How much weight do the analysis, the recommendations and the presentation get?" | Spend time where the points are |
| J3 | "**Format**: how long is the presentation, how long is the Q&A, how many slides max? Are the 4 deliverables graded separately?" | Avoid losing points on form |
| J4 | "What did **past winning teams** do that others did not?" | The implicit expectations |
| J5 | "Can we **contact Sense Reply again** before the final, or interview external experts?" | A second interview = real data, a strong differentiator |
| J6 | "Can we **use the B2 team's results** directly in our business case?" | A quantified business case with real pilot data |
| J7 | "Do they prefer **one bold recommendation** or a balanced overview of options?" | Shapes the storyline |

## Part 3 — Questions our team must answer before the final deck

If we cannot answer one of these in one sentence, the deck is not ready.

1. **Where should Sense Reply send its sales team next month?** Name a segment, a country and ideally 3 target companies.
2. **Why them, and why now?** Which trigger (regulation, deadline, market) makes this urgent?
3. **Who exactly signs, who blocks, and how do we get past the blocker?**
4. **Product or service?** Our answer, with the number that justifies it.
5. **What does the client pay, for what, and how does it grow over time?**
6. **Who is the closest alternative, and why do we beat it** (in the client's words, not ours)?
7. **What is the biggest risk** to our recommendation (AWS, AI Act, long DSO cycles, cannibalisation), and what is the mitigation?
8. **What happens in the first 90 days?** A concrete pilot proposal: client type, duration, price, success KPI.
9. **What would make us change our mind?** Which data, if it came back different, would flip the recommendation?
10. **What does the CEO remember after 3 minutes?** One sentence, one number, one next step.

## How to use these questions in the meeting

- **Open with A1 and A2.** They set the frame and show seniority.
- **B2 (last deal won / lost)** is the single most valuable question: let them talk, and ask "why?" twice.
- When they answer "it depends", ask **"on your last client, what was it?"**
- **Say our hypotheses out loud.** A client correcting a hypothesis gives more information than a client answering an open question.
- **Write down their exact words.** A client quote on a slide ("Our clients want to save money, not the planet") carries more weight with the jury than our own claim.
- Close with: **"If you were us, what would you put on slide 1?"**
